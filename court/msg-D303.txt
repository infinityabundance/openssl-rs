rand: the seeding arm lands, and its entropy comes from the kernel on both tests (D303)

providers/implementations/rands/seeding/rand_unix.c, integrated as src/rand/unix.rs:
ossl_pool_acquire_entropy's two arms on this profile (OPENSSL_RAND_SEED_OS expands to
GETRANDOM + DEVRANDOM), ossl_pool_add_nonce_data, ossl_rand_pool_init/_cleanup and
_keep_random_devices_open, the /dev device cache with wait_random_seeded and the
SysV-shm probe, and syscall_random's getentropy-then-getrandom shape. Suite 644 -> 648.

This is the unit D298 moved: ossl_pool_acquire_entropy and the pool init/cleanup are
here, not in crypto/rand/rand_pool.c, which is why D287's table named the wrong file
for 9.5. The arm reaches the kernel for entropy, so the tests are the difference
between "transcribed" and "works here" -- and they run the real path:

  acquire_entropy_fills_the_pool_from_the_kernel   the pool acquires from the kernel
                                                   and holds at least min_len bytes, and
                                                   32 bytes from getrandom(2) are not
                                                   all zero
  a_second_acquisition_succeeds_...                a second pool re-runs the arm, which
                                                   is what exercises the device cache
  add_nonce_data_appends_...                       the nonce is appended, not XORed
  init_is_idempotent_...                           init answers 1 twice and the
                                                   keep-open flag does not change that

Every libc call goes through src/rand/sys.rs, landed and tested in D302; this module
declares no extern block of its own.

Three things the integration found, each recorded at the site:

1. A wrong expectation of MINE, corrected against the authority: the RAND_POOL_426 and
   RAND_POOL_431 arms ("buffer is null", "length is zero") belong to
   ossl_rand_pool_adin_mix_in, NOT to ossl_pool_add_nonce_data. add_nonce_data ends in
   ossl_rand_pool_add, which appends whatever fits -- so a zero-length pool accepts a
   nonce and grows by the struct's size. The test first asserted a refusal and failed;
   the transcription was right, and both behaviours are now asserted against the
   function that has each.
2. syscall_random's getentropy probe cannot be reproduced: the authority declares
   getentropy __attribute__((weak)) so libcrypto links where it is absent, and Rust has
   no weak-symbol mechanism. The binding is strong, the divergence is stated on the
   declaration in src/rand/sys.rs, and no weaker shim was invented.
3. fd_set's masked index is the one behavioural divergence in the platform layer, and
   it is asserted as such rather than left to be discovered.
