The platform layer is the one worth reading, because every value in it was checked
rather than recalled: __NR_getrandom is 318, read from /usr/include/asm/unistd_64.h:322
and corroborated by the authority's own #if at rand_unix.c:283 (not the asm-generic 278
and not x32); struct stat is 144 bytes with st_dev at 0, st_ino at 8 and st_mode at 24,
read from bits/struct_stat.h's __x86_64__ arm; dev_t/ino_t/mode_t are 8/8/4 from
bits/typesizes.h; fd_set is sixteen 64-bit words with __FD_SETSIZE 1024, from sys/select.h
and bits/select.h; ENOSYS is 38 from asm-generic/errno.h.

Five more corrections and measurements, all from reading the tree:

1. There is no RAND_FILE_NAME_MAX and no RANDFILE constant anywhere in 3.6.4. "RANDFILE"
   exists only as the literal environment-variable *name*. The seeding of the default RNG
   from $RANDFILE is apps/lib/app_rand.c, not randfile.c.
2. rand_uniform.c has no _uint64 variant. It has exactly two functions, both uint32.
3. randfile.c needs sys::{stat, fstat, fdopen, chmod, setbuf, clearerr} and the three-arg
   open, so D298's list of what the random layer needs from the platform was short by six.
4. The authority probes getentropy as a *weak* symbol. Rust has no weak-symbol mechanism,
   so the binding here is strong and the divergence is stated on the declaration rather
   than hidden: the authority links on a host without getentropy, this crate would not.
5. open is declared variadic, because that is the <fcntl.h> prototype and it serves the
   two-argument /dev/* call sites and the three-argument randfile.c site with one honest
   signature. Integration replaces the crate's existing two-argument open rather than
   adding beside it, which the binding's own doc says.
