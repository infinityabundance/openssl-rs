## The openssh graduation attempt, and why it has not graduated

`openssh` imports only `libcrypto`; neither `ssh` nor `sshd` links `libssl`, so
nothing in this section is a claim about TLS, sessions or the record layer.

### What was achieved

The closure was driven from **0 discharged / 15 blocked / 105 unknown** to
**1 obligation, discharged, 0 refusals, `ACCEPTED`**, with every reachable file
enumerated and **0 target-bounded unresolved sites**. Approximately 40 vestigial
`unsafe` markers were removed because their bodies read none of their
arguments — reduction, not documentation.

Real defects were found and fixed:

| defect | class |
|---|---|
| `pk7_cb` declared `boundary`/`ndef_bio` in the wrong order, so `BIO_new_NDEF` returned an `ASN1_OCTET_STRING`'s data address as a `BIO` | type confusion |
| over-aligned types could obtain a misaligned `NonNull<T>` through **safe** code | soundness |
| SSLv3 master-secret digest temporaries uncleansed on early-failure returns | §5 |
| six legacy digest initialisers wrote through a NULL `md_data`; the **authority segfaults** (139) | UB vs §17 |
| BLAKE2 `compress` held a whole-context `&mut` and a `buf` pointer; **Stacked Borrows UB** | aliasing |
| the closure's 64 KiB switch scan **invented** reachability edges | resolver correctness |

The BLAKE2 repair is the strongest of these: `compress` now takes disjoint
`&mut h`, `&mut t`, `&f` and a `*const` block pointer, so the conflicting
whole-context reference is **inexpressible** rather than merely absent.

### Why it has not graduated

Two independent adjudications **rejected** it, and the rejections are the most
valuable output of the exercise. Both failed on the same property, at three
levels:

- the receipt that accepted the closure was derived against install
  `libcrypto c2d73687` while the rebuild at the adjudicated commit was
  `be578c86`, so **the analysed DSO did not contain the source it described**;
- the **executed** consumer binaries (`23c260e9`, `d9e92604`) are not the
  **analysed** ones (`c8723f34`, `dccccf07`), and the runtime rows carry no
  consumer digest at all;
- discharge receipts drifted 4–82 lines off their operations, one was anchored
  at a line that is now a struct field, and one of the 28 contract-bounded sites
  was mislabelled as a provider entry when it is an in-image callback.

The root cause is procedural, not analytical: **every cycle rebuilds, and the
ledger keeps pointing at the previous build**, because a later subphase edits
source after the measurement it then argues about.

### The one procedure that can pass

A single frozen cycle with no edits between steps: fix the two mislabels, then
freeze the source, rebuild once, and in that same freeze regenerate the
inventory, the closure, the receipts **and** the consumer measurements, binding
the executed binary digest into the runtime row; then adjudicate against that
freeze untouched. Elements 1, 2 and 5 of the predicate are one property — *the
thing analysed is the thing measured is the thing executed* — and no amount of
analysis substitutes for it.

### What the claim would not cover, even when it passes

Workload-specific parity only (the named `ssh` and `sshd` workloads); no TLS
claim; no other consumer; the other 66 crate-wide production blockers, which are
excluded as unreachable rather than proven safe; the static reachability model
with its contract-bounded provider and allocator seams; and no
cryptographic/timing assurance.

`SAFE_COMPATIBLE` remains **0/33**.
