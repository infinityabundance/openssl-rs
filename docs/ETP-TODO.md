# Earned Trust Protocol — work queue

Single tracked queue for the assurance programme. Nothing in this file is a
claim: `DONE` means the artefact exists in Git at the named commit, `PARTIAL`
means a bounded piece exists with named residues, everything else is open.

Status vocabulary for each item: `TODO`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `PARTIAL`, `DONE`,
`BLOCKED` (with the blocker), `NOT_APPLICABLE` (with the reason).

Rules that bind this queue (from AGENTS.md, extended by the ETP mandate):

- a claim may never exceed its evidence; an implementation is not a specification;
- a test is not a proof; source-level verification is not binary-level verification;
- compatibility is not security; memory safety is not constant-time execution;
- a reproducible build is not a correct build; a signature establishes provenance,
  not truth;
- an independent agent is not an independent organisation;
- absence of observed failure is not universal absence;
- an undocumented assumption is an unresolved obligation;
- a counterexample outranks a thousand successes within its scope;
- an instrument must demonstrate sensitivity to the defect it claims to detect;
- historical evidence is never fresh evidence;
- no authority, including the project principal, may promote a contradicted claim
  by declaration;
- **do not omit, do not stop, push often.**

## I. Non-claims that bind this queue

SARIF compliance does not prove absence of vulnerabilities. A clean scanner
output is not memory safety. A signed report is not necessarily true. DSSE does
not establish independence. A transparency entry does not establish
completeness. SLSA provenance alone does not establish a reproducible build. A
reproducible build is not a correct build. Two signatures are not necessarily
two independent verifications. A cryptographically valid historical signature
does not establish present authorisation. A signed summary does not replace the
underlying proof. A successful CI workflow is not regulatory certification.

Addendum B (§B.32) adds: transparency inclusion does not prove software
correctness; one signed checkpoint is not a globally consistent history; two log
instances are not two independent operators; a witness has not reviewed the
implementation; witnessing does not prove all evidence was disclosed; a
timestamp does not prove an experiment occurred; a public commitment does not
establish availability of its payload; a transparency log is not a revocation
system; an authentic historical assertion may be currently invalid; experimental
post-quantum features are not production post-quantum security; registration in a
public-good log is not endorsement by its operator.

## J. Performance Frontier Programme (separate queue)

Normative programme: `docs/PERFORMANCE-FRONTIER.md`. Work queue:
`docs/PERFORMANCE-FRONTIER-TODO.md`, items `PFP-0` … `PFP-17`.

This is a separate queue because it is a distinct concern with its own governing
standard, not a re-lettering of sections B–G. Its bindings on this queue are:

- every performance result is an ETP claim under `docs/ETP-CLAIM-ALGEBRA.md` and
  carries a candidate identity, instrument identity, population, scope, threat
  model and obligations;
- performance classifications (`MEASURED`, `COMPETITIVE`, `PARITY_ESTABLISHED`,
  `AHEAD`, `FRONTIER_LEADING`, `INDEPENDENTLY_REPRODUCED_FRONTIER`) are disjoint
  from `SAFE_COMPATIBLE` and from semantic assurance;
- a faster arm without the differential courts and the binary constant-time
  audit at the same digest is an experiment, not a candidate;
- `PFP-1` and `PFP-2` overlap the open timing obligations T23, T24 and T29's
  residuals; a performance result never discharges a timing obligation, and a
  timing obligation is never traded for speed;
- a result measured on the virtualised court is `SCOPED_OBSERVATION` and is not
  a frontier result; `FRONTIER_LEADING` requires bare metal with a PMU on every
  platform the claim covers.

Nothing in section J alters the curl consumer's position as last.

## K. Formal Refinement and Certification (separate queue)

Normative programme: `docs/FORMAL-REFINEMENT-CERTIFICATION.md`. Work queue:
`docs/FRC-TODO.md`, items `FRC-0` … `FRC-10`.

A third separate queue, for the same reason as section J: a distinct concern
with its own standard. Its bindings on this queue:

- every FRC result is an ETP claim and inherits the claim algebra's refusal
  rules; an FRC item never upgrades an ETP verdict by itself, it supplies
  evidence the verdict may cite;
- matching the frontier is a precondition for claiming to exceed it, and the
  property matched and the property added must both be named;
- a proof in a model is not a proof about the deployed binary, and a binary
  timing result is not a physical side-channel guarantee;
- a specification is never generated by restating the implementation, and no
  obligation is closed by weakening a requirement;
- DO-178C objective satisfaction is recorded as such and never as certification;
- `PFP` optimisation and `FRC` evidence interact in one direction: an
  optimisation may proceed only while the FRC evidence for the property it could
  affect remains valid at the new digest, and any change to a named kernel,
  specification or toolchain invalidates the claim.

Section K runs after every item in sections D, E, F and J. It is the final
assurance track, not an interleaved one, because its frontier-gap register and
specification hierarchy consume their outputs. The FRC queue therefore starts
empty of execution and is picked up when sections D, E, F and J are complete.
The curl consumer remains the final consumer item.
