## 1. What this stratum is, and what it is not

Phase 21 is the stratum `docs/RELEASE_GATES.md` §1 names "Maintenance delta machinery", the
procedure §8's "Future version policy" requires. Like Phases 16 through 20 it owns **no exported
symbol**: `forensics/atlas/symbol-ownership.json` assigns `owner_phase == 21` nothing, and
`forensics/atlas/provider-algorithms.json` assigns it no registration row. Its unit is the
**maintenance delta contract** — five contract units, each measured by the court that lands with
it — and it hands nothing forward and receives nothing, because the two authorities whose delta it
computes are already admitted, their differential is already committed, and the implementation it
re-measures is the one Phases 3 through 20 completed. It adds no crate code.

What it owes is the **delta procedure made mechanical** over the two authorities already on disk:
an **authority admission** (`RT-AUTHORITY-ADMISSION`), an **atlas delta** (`RT-ATLAS-DELTA`), a
**delta disposition** (`RT-DELTA-DISPOSITION`), an **affected-court selection**
(`RT-AFFECTED-COURT-SELECTION`) and a **maintenance-boundary register**
(`MAINTENANCE-BOUNDARY-REGISTER`). All five are in `artifacts/phase21/COURTS.json` and pass.

**A passing delta court is an instrument, not a property.** Each unit records `measurement_state`
(the instrument ran and its control was honest) beside `property_status` and `findings` (what, if
anything, it claims about the delta property). `delta-disposition` is the unit where the plan
says the two diverge, and §3 is where that is stated rather than buried.

**The affected-court selection.** From the dispositioned delta and the coverage atlas's

## 3. The instrument-versus-property split, and how the seal resolves it

`docs/PHASE-21-SUBPHASES.md` §1 names `delta-disposition` as the unit where the two axes diverge:
a passing `RT-DELTA-DISPOSITION` records, as `findings`, every delta row it could not disposition,
so its property reads `NOT_CLAIMED` with `findings_present`. **On this tree the court records zero
findings** — every target is dispositioned (2 `implemented`, 3 `boundary`, 0 unexplained) — so the
unit's property reads `not_claimed` and `findings_present` is false. That is the disposition
being complete for the tree, not the instrument being silent: an un-dispositioned row is a `fail`
and its finding would move the property to `NOT_CLAIMED`. The ledger reads both axes from the
court row rather than typing them, and the invariant
`forensics/tools/phase21_obligations.py` enforces (the same shape every stratum uses) would carry
a finding the moment one existed.

**A passing `RT-DELTA-DISPOSITION` must never be read as "the delta is implemented".** The court's
pass says the instrument ran, its evidence is committed and its control detected the injected
gaps; the property it names is the completeness of the disposition over the delta the atlas-delta
court computed, and no more. The claim the seal licenses is the bounded one §5 states.

## 4. What the courts do not establish — and the surfaces that are not measured

A capability an instrument does not drive is not observed by it, and a plane its inputs cannot
compare is **named `not-measured` rather than counted as motionless** (`docs/PHASE-21-SUBPHASES.md`
§3.3). Three surfaces of the delta procedure the plan names are **not measured on this tree**;
each is stated here with the reason and with what would make it measurable, and each is carried as
a bounded register row rather than buried.

- **provider registration rows — `not-measured`.** The procedure names
  `forensics/atlas/provider-algorithms.json`, the openssl-rs census of the *production* authority's
  provider tables. No historical counterpart is committed (`forensics/atlas/openssl-3.6.3-historical`
  carries only `provider-inventory.json`) and `atlas_differential.py` does not compare that census,
  so the named authority-to-authority registration-row delta cannot be computed from committed
  data. **What would make it measurable:** a second `provider-algorithms.json` census generated
  over the historical authority's provider tables, so the two authorities' registration rows can
  be set-diffed. The differential *does* compare the per-authority `provider-inventory.json` under
  its own `provider_algorithms` key; the court measures that **adjacent** plane (**0** added,
  **0** removed) and records it as adjacent, never as the named plane, so the unmeasured plane is
  not counted motionless.
- **prerequisite units — `not-measured`.** The procedure names `forensics/prerequisites.json`,
  which is a single-authority openssl-rs artefact (the implementation's prerequisite and
  divergence units, not an authority's), with no committed historical counterpart and no
  differential tool that compares it, so the authority-to-authority unit delta cannot be computed.
  Its shape is recorded for context (1 deferral, 14 divergences, 152 units) but it is not a delta.
  **What would make it measurable:** a committed historical counterpart to that plane (the
  authority's own prerequisite/divergence units) and a differential tool that compares the two.
- **declaration bodies — the `declarations.*.changed` axis, `not-measured`.** The measured exports
  plane compares declared-surface **membership by key**, not declaration bodies, so a declaration
  whose body moved without its name moving is invisible. The ABI symbols' `changed` axis **is**
  measured (their DSO version); the *declared* declarations' `changed` axis is not. **What would
  make it measurable:** a differential that compares declaration bodies (their values), not
  membership alone.

Beyond the unmeasured surfaces, the courts do not establish:

- **No re-run was performed.** `RT-AFFECTED-COURT-SELECTION` re-derives the selected courts from
  the registries each already published; it does not re-execute their probes (`reexecuted_courts`
  0). It records which courts `docs/RELEASE_GATES.md` §8's "re-run affected courts" step would
  run, and that the selected courts still stand, not that a re-run happened.
- **The three boundary rows are boundaries, not motionless.** The delta's `not-measured` plane and
  axis rows are recorded, not resolved; on the current tree `unexplained` is 0 because every row
  is dispositioned, and an un-dispositioned row would be a finding and a `fail`, never a silent
  addition.
- **The register claims four surfaces and bounds six; it does not claim the bounded ones.**

## 6. FRF and Gemel: the chain entry, and why it is vacuous

**This stratum owns no FRF-declarable court, so it begins no FRF chain and needs no Gemel
checkpoint.** All five courts stage no `artifacts/phase21/probes/<probe>.{authority,candidate}`
pair — they read committed evidence — so each carries `frf_declarable: false` with its exclusion
reason in `artifacts/phase21/COURTS.json`. `forensics/tools/phase_state.py`'s
`frf_gemel_blocking_reason` derives its requirement from that inventory, never from the registry
it checks, and its documented rule is that *"a stratum whose own court inventory declares no
FRF-declarable court has begun no chain and is **not blocked here**."* Confirmed on this tree:
`frf_gemel_blocking_reason(21) == ""`. This is the same scoping under which Phase 20 — the
custodian seal — and Phase 22 — the other non-declarable meta-stratum — sealed without a chain
entry. Unlike Phases 19 and 20, this stratum's plan §4.2 carries no `gen_frf_courts.py` registry
clause; the only occurrence of that clause is the runner docstring, which Phase 20's own seal left
in place, so no plan correction is recorded for it.

No `.frf` object and no Gemel change or checkpoint was created for Phase 21, because the
mechanism requires none. The FRF store's chain status is recorded here rather than moved: the
store carries **21 compiled claims with zero blockers** — the **17** sensitivity-backed claims
(one per runtime stratum, Phases 3 through 19) each recording candidate `openssl-rs 0.0.27`
(`identity_hash e4f60d8b`) plus the abi/dgst/inventory/baseline oracle claims — over **147**
receipts, and `forensics/GEMEL_TRAJECTORY.md`'s `current:` is the **0.0.27** release checkpoint
`K70` (`checkpoint.4b913a19e54b61b2f21bf0ed78a152424a79e6dbd291684b2b5f4dc0d713d35d`), closing
change `C116`. The store is **re-created at a release, not at a stratum seal**: the `0.0.27`
release removed and rebuilt it at the new version, folded the now-complete Phases 22 and 23 into the

Phases 20, 21 and 22 (the `0.0.26` release commit `f75f6879`; the `0.0.25` release commit
`ad6fd709` did the same for Phase 20). This seal records the division: **the stratum records
the chain status; the release re-creates the store.**

## 7. Non-claims

* **No version universality.** OpenSSL 4.x is a new compatibility profile, and a 3.x receipt is
  never silently reinterpreted as evidence for 4 (`docs/RELEASE_GATES.md` §8,
  `docs/NON_CLAIMS.md` §3). Both of this stratum's authorities are 3.6.x, so the trajectory it
  measures is a maintenance delta *within one compatibility profile* and a passing court cannot
  widen it.
* **Only the exercised delta is claimed.** The claim is exactly the movement between the two
  authorities it names; the absence of movement elsewhere is not measured and no court may
  generalise it (`docs/PHASE-21-SUBPHASES.md` §0, §4.3).
* **Unknown stays unknown.** `docs/PARITY_MODEL.md` §1 makes `UNKNOWN` distinct from `FAIL` and a
  research result, never a resting state; an un-dispositioned delta row is `UNKNOWN`, and a
  passing court does not resolve it.
* **No security-fix reintroduction.** A 3.6.3 behaviour that corresponds to an upstream security
  fix is not reintroduced: the delta runs historical → production and follows the fixed authority,
  and the disposition court's `removed`/`changed` axis enforces the prohibition
  (`docs/SECURITY_DIVERGENCE_POLICY.md` §1, §3).
* **The claim is bounded** by the two authorities, the exports plane and the surfaces named, and
  by the two planes and one axis §4 records as **not measured**, each with what would make it
  measurable.
