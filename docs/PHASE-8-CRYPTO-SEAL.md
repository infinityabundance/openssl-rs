## 1. What this phase owns, and how that was decided

Phase 8 is **the primitives**: the low-level cryptographic constructions the rest of `libcrypto`
is built out of, and the provider implementations that publish them. It is deliberately *not* the
plumbing. Phase 7's framework fetches, caches and dispatches a method; this stratum supplies the
methods, and the two are related exactly as the authority's own layering relates them.

**The working set is derived, not chosen.** `forensics/atlas/symbol-ownership.json` gives every one
of the authority's exports exactly one owning stratum, and this stratum's projection is the set the
plan's §1 and `forensics/tools/phase8_obligations.py`'s rule both read:

> the stratum's working set is the projection of `forensics/atlas/symbol-ownership.json` for phase 8,
> plus every symbol an earlier stratum's ledger records as handed to it

That second clause is why the ledger's working set is larger than the atlas projection: **Phase 7
handed this stratum twenty-seven symbols** — the twelve legacy low-level-key accessors
(`EVP_PKEY_get0_*`/`get1_*` for `RSA`, `DH`, `DSA`, `EC_KEY`, `hmac`, `poly1305`, `siphash`),
`EVP_PKEY_assign`, the `EVP_PKEY_encrypt_old`/`decrypt_old` pair, three `d2i_` spellings and the three
`EVP_PKEY_meth_*` readers — each of which is declared in a header this stratum owns but was reached
first through the framework. They landed here, and
`forensics/phase8-obligations.json`'s `handoffs_discharged` records the edge rather than retiring it
(D353). The split is `docs/SEAL-CENSUS.md`'s per-stratum table.

**The nine subphases, and the one that is not a subphase.** `docs/PHASE-8-SUBPHASES.md` §2 divides
the work as 8.1 digests, 8.2 symmetric ciphers, 8.3 AEAD and modes, 8.4 RSA, 8.5 DH/DHX, 8.6 DSA,
8.7 EC, 8.8 the `crypto/asn1/ameth_lib.c` method objects and `standard_methods[]`, 8.9 the `pem.h`
key helpers, and 8.10 the seal — this document. The provider *registration* rows are not a subphase of
their own; they are the rows the subphases' units publish, and the four courts `RT-KEYMGMT`,
`RT-SIGNATURE`, `RT-ASYM-CIPHER` and `RT-PROVIDER-CAP` exist because a row that is `implemented` but
never *driven* satisfies a coverage join without saying anything about whether it dispatches.

**The two planes, which is this stratum's central methodological commitment (D201).** Every
primitive-bearing subphase carries **two** courts, and neither replaces the other. An `RT-*` court is
**differential**: the same probe compiled twice, against the authority and against the candidate, with
the two transcripts diffed line by line, so it answers *"does the candidate behave like the
authority?"* A `CT-*` court is a **correctness** court: the probe is compiled once against the
candidate alone and compared with committed expected bytes whose provenance is recorded per vector, so
it answers *"is the construction right?"*. The distinction is not pedantry and it was paid for: a
published vector proves the construction is *some* correct implementation, and a differential pass
proves it is *this* implementation's observable behaviour. A row can pass one and fail the other, and
§4 records each time that happened.

## 2. What has been built

**8.1, the digests.** The low-level `X_Init`/`_Update`/`_Final`/`_Transform` API and the provider
`OSSL_OP_DIGEST` rows, for MD4, MD5, MDC2, RIPEMD-160, Whirlpool, SM3, SHA-1, SHA-2, SHA-3/KECCAK/SHAKE
and BLAKE2 (D204, D207). The C-tables are *generated* from the authority rather than transcribed —
`forensics/tools/gen_phase8_tables.py` re-derives them and checks the result — because a
transcription of a 256-entry table is a transcription that can be wrong silently.

**8.2, the symmetric ciphers.** AES in all its modes, DES/3DES, RC2, RC4, Blowfish, CAST5, IDEA, SEED
and Camellia at the low level, plus the `OSSL_OP_CIPHER` provider rows over the shared engines
`ciphercommon.c.in`/`ciphercommon_hw.c`/`ciphercommon_block.c` (D209–D223). SM4 and ARIA have **no
low-level API** in this authority — it exports no `SM4_*`/`ARIA_*` — so their primitives are internals
with no export (D266, D268) and their rows belong to 8.3.

**8.3, AEAD and modes.** All fifty `modes.h` exports — key wrap, GCM, CCM, XTS, OCB — and the provider
rows above them: the twelve AES key-wrap rows, the six CBC-CTS rows, AES-XTS, AES-OCB, AES-CCM, the
thirteen `AES-*-CBC-HMAC-*` rows, the AES-GCM-SIV and AES-SIV constructions, `ChaCha20`,
`ChaCha20-Poly1305`, the five `SM4-*` and twenty-one `ARIA-*` rows, and the nine `OSSL_OP_MAC` rows
(D224–D281). Three of the constructions here are **stitchings of primitives already in** rather than
fourth primitives — SIV, GCM-SIV and ChaCha20-Poly1305 — and each one is where a defect that no
round-trip test can see is possible, which is what §4 records.

**8.4–8.7, the four key families.** RSA, DH/DHX, DSA and EC: the arithmetic, the `keymgmt` rows, the
signature, exchange, asym-cipher and KEM rows over them, and the encoding helpers
(D340–D353, D387–D393, D396, D406, D410). The EC arithmetic includes the generated curve tables
(`forensics/tools/gen_ec_curves.py`) and the curve25519/curve448 precomputation.

**8.8 and 8.9, the method objects and the PEM key helpers.** `crypto/asn1/ameth_lib.c`'s
`standard_methods[]` and the twelve legacy method objects it names, which is the table that made
`EVP_PKEY_type`, `EVP_PKEY_meth_find` and the fifteen `src/asn1/ameth.rs` rows a *cycle* until it
landed (D341, D353, D369); and `crypto/pem/pem_pkey.c`'s read and write halves (D318, D369).

**8.10 and this pass — the provider capability surface, which had no owner.** The last thing Phase 8
owed was not an export and not a registration row: it was the default provider's *capability* face.
`providers/common/capabilities.c` (344 lines) defines `ossl_prov_get_capabilities`, and
`defltprov.c` binds it — together with `deflt_gettable_params` and `deflt_get_params` — into the
provider's dispatch table. It is in **neither ledger**: not an exported symbol, and not a row of
`defltprov.c`'s algorithm tables. That is exactly why nothing objected to its absence for the
stratum's whole life (D410's own words, now discharged by **D411**): the evidence machinery had no
row to be unhappy about. `src/provider/capabilities.rs` transcribes the unit whole — the function,
its two walkers and its four static tables, at their full sizes because every `#if` in the authority
guards on a macro this profile leaves undefined — and `src/provider/digest.rs` gains the two provider
parameter functions and three dispatch entries, taking `DEFLT_DISPATCH` from two arms plus a
terminator to five plus a terminator. `RT-PROVIDER-CAP` drives all of it in §3.

## 4. What the courts found

A court whose results never surprised anyone is a court that is not looking, and this stratum's two
planes found real defects, including one in the evidence machinery itself. Recorded here because each
one is the reason a later reader should trust the court rather than the prose.

**The differential plane found defects no round trip can see.** `ChaCha20-Poly1305`'s TLS-AAD arm
rebinds `tohash` to the keystream buffer, and a transcription that only zeroed a length hashed
sixteen untouched bytes instead of the length block: the **ciphertext was right and the tag was
wrong**, which no encrypt/decrypt round trip and no ciphertext comparison can observe. `RT-CIPHER`
caught it because it compares the authority's own record (D279). The same court found that every
provider cipher row's `newctx`/`dupctx`/`freectx` reported one constant source file where the
authority's own allocation traffic records a different one per row, and `RT-CIPHER-MEM` now prints
the distinct `providers/implementations/ciphers/` file per row over all 131 rows (D280).

**The correctness plane found what the differential plane cannot.** `CT-DIGEST`'s first run failed
exactly the three digest constructions the work in progress already suspected were wrong, which is
the property that makes it a court rather than a restatement (D201); the second plane then found a
fourth defect the first could not (D204). `CT-ML-DSA` is **ninety published FIPS 204 / ACVP vectors**
and closed the ML-DSA rows that a differential court could only have agreed with (D409). The SM2 rows
are the clearest instance of the two planes disagreeing in the useful direction: the differential
court passed and the published vectors **failed**, and the vectors were right (D406).

**The courts found a memory-safety defect in this crate.** `hpke_labeled_expand` computed its `info`
buffer length once as the over-large allocation bound, then dropped the correction — so it handed
`hpke_kdf_expand` the allocation bound rather than the bytes actually written, and every `LabelExpand`
consumed uninitialised heap. A direct in-crate round trip **passed**; only the court's two-process
diff exposed it, because both halves read the same allocation (D410). The repair is in, and the arm
that found it was strengthened — to print digests — rather than loosened.

**The courts found a defect in the evidence machinery, twice.** D320 measured that `blocker_liveness`
was never run over a row whose blocker is another *stratum's* export, which is why Phase 9's row
stayed "blocked" through `RAND_bytes_ex` landing without anything objecting. D412 measured that the
seal map had **two divergent copies** — `render_seal_census.py` knew phases 3–7 and `phase_state.py`
knew only phases 1–2 — so `docs/PHASE-3-CORE-RUNTIME-SEAL.md` through
`docs/PHASE-7-EVP-SEAL.md` were on disk, the census named every one, and `forensics/phase-state.json`
recorded `seal_sha256: null` for all five. Both were found only because a later pass needed the
machinery to answer a question.

**And the FRF venue found a defect the court venue could not (D413).** `courts/phase8/rt_ec_probe.c`
declared `EC_builtin_curve one[2]` without an initialiser and then printed `one[1]` as "the sentinel
it still is" — a sentinel the comment described and the code never set. The court venue reads `0` on
both sides and passes; FRF's venue read `0` against `26` and raised a residual on `RT-EC`'s stdout
surface. It is an **uninitialised stack read in this project's own probe**, not a divergence in the
library, and it is the class `forensics/tools/probe_hygiene.py` exists to catch. The probe now
declares an explicit `{ -1, NULL }` sentinel, `-1` being a NID no builtin curve row carries, so the
arm observes what it claims. §8 records what the residual cost and how it was dispositioned.

**Two measured corrections to the plan's own prose.** D320 found that
`ossl_ifc_ffc_compute_security_bits` and the existing `BN_security_bits` agree at 2048 bits and
**disagree at 4096 (152 against 128)** — reusing the existing function, the obvious move, would have
answered a wrong security strength for every 4096-bit FFC group. And D318 found that no existing
`RT-RSA` arm covered a single export of the RSA object layer, which is a coverage gap the atlas could
not see because the atlas joins on *names a probe references*, not on arms.

## 5. Fault boundaries — recorded, not reproduced

Where the authority dereferences a NULL, relies on an uninitialised field, or **aborts**, the court
does not call it and `docs/SECURITY_DIVERGENCE_POLICY.md` records the divergence with the phase or
condition that would make the behaviour reachable, so the record retires with that phase rather than
with a re-reading of this document. The register's entries whose subject code is Phase 8's:

- **`D-CBCHMAC-MULTIBLOCK-ENC-1`** — the four `AES-*-CBC-HMAC-*` rows' multiblock *encrypt*
  parameter is not driven, because its IVs come from the random layer and `crypto/rand/` is Phase 9's.
  The row answers 0 with no error queued, which is what the authority's own code does when
  `RAND_bytes_ex` fails. Its sibling observables (AAD, max-buffer-size, and a whole TLS 1.0 record in
  both directions) **are** reached by `RT-CIPHER`.
- **`D-CBCHMAC-MAXBUFSZ-ASSERT-1`** — `tls1multi_maxbufsz` before `maxsndfrag` **aborts the authority**
  (measured, exit 134). The crate computes and answers 53 where the authority aborts. This one is
  permanent and deliberate: it is a safety divergence, not a gap.
- **`D-EC-1`, superseded by `D-EC-2`** — `curve_list[]`'s method column is not transcribed, because
  its one non-NULL value is a perlasm-only method. Recording a `None` in that column was tried and
  rejected: it would be a **fabricated value**, indistinguishable at the ABI from a real one.
- **`D-EC-2`** — and this is the stratum's one **trigger-bearing, genuinely unreached** item, stated
  plainly rather than folded away: the `NID_X9_62_prime256v1` row resolves to `EC_GFp_simple_method`
  where the authority answers `EC_GFp_nistz256_method`, because `crypto/ec/ecp_nistz256.c` is a
  2,328-line unit whose implementation in this authority is **perlasm** and is not transcribed. Three
  named consequences follow, and they are consequences rather than omissions: the
  `EC_GROUP_method_of` comparison is **not** carried as an `RT-EC` arm (a court's verdict is `pass`
  only when residuals are empty, so it cannot carry a known difference);
  `EC_nistz256_pre_comp_dup`/`_free` are not given a module, unreachable while no nistz256 group can
  be constructed; and `EC_GROUP_have_precompute_mult` answers 0 where the authority answers 1, so
  `RT-EC` skips that NID. `forensics/prerequisites.json` carries the record machine-readably with
  `owner_module` `src/ec/curve.rs`, `class` `modelled_differently`.
- **`D-PKEY-AMETH-1`, `-2`, superseded by `-3`, which D372 supersedes in turn** — the register's
  entries for the legacy method objects' `find` functions answering NULL. Every one is closed: D372
  landed the ECX chain and the register's third entry retires with it. These are recorded here because
  a superseded divergence is still part of what a reader must be able to find, and because the
  supersession is itself the evidence that the omission was repaired rather than forgotten.
- **Withdrawn: `D-PKEYCTX-LEGACY-ALG-1`.** The register marks it as a claim that never existed —
  "there is no behavioural difference between the authority and the candidate at this site". It is
  named so that a reader who finds it in the register's history knows it was withdrawn rather than
  resolved.

**Observation counts quoted inside older decision and divergence entries are the values current when
those entries were written** and are not this document's counts. `docs/SEAL-CENSUS.md` and
`artifacts/phase8/COURTS.json` are authoritative; a stale count inside a historical record is a stale
sentence, not a missing court. (`D-EC-2` cites `RT-EC` at "2134 observations", `D-EC-1` at "483" and
`D-PKEY-AMETH-3` at "232"; none matches §3, and none is meant to.)

## 6. What is explicitly NOT claimed

1. **Not parity, and not a usable OpenSSL.** `implemented` in the ledgers means a symbol with that
   name is defined. `docs/PARITY_MODEL.md` states what each label means; `forensics/STATUS.md` carries
   the current figures. No symbol here is `PARITY_VERIFIED`.
2. **Nothing cryptographic.** The `RT-*` courts compare answers with the authority's; the `CT-*`
   courts compare with published vectors. Neither establishes that any construction is sound,
   constant-time or side-channel resistant, and **no formal validation is claimed**.
3. **A passing court is a differential result over the arms that court drives.** `probe_hygiene.py`
   and each probe's own "deliberately not observed" list are what make the gap visible rather than
   inferred.
4. **`libssl` is untouched.** Every one of its exports remains `SCAFFOLDED` and aborts when called.
5. **Nothing about a build profile other than the admitted one.** `no-deprecated`, the legacy
   provider and FIPS-capable configurations are separate authorities that have not been built. The
   digest and cipher families this stratum owns are all compiled **in**; `OPENSSL_NO_DEPRECATED` is
   *not* defined, so every `OSSL_DEPRECATEDIN_3_0` declaration in `md4.h`, `md5.h`, `sha.h`,
   `ripemd.h`, `whrlpool.h`, `mdc2.h`, `rsa.h`, `dh.h`, `dsa.h` and `ec.h` is compiled and is an
   obligation the ledger carries.
6. **Nothing about a platform other than Linux x86-64**, and nothing about a profile where assembly
   is disabled. §5's `D-EC-2` is the standing consequence: where this authority's implementation is
   perlasm, this crate's is a portable reconstruction, and a differential court says so arm by arm.
7. **Nothing about the `base` and `null` providers.** They remain absent **by design**, and the
   coordinate that says so is the census rather than prose:
   `forensics/atlas/provider-algorithms.json`'s `projection` block reads `open["8"] == 0`, and the 319
   rows those two files define carry `owning_phase` 10 (318) and 9 (1). A derived `complete` must not
   be read as their having landed. Three smaller provider-side items are absent for the same reason
   and are named in `src/provider/digest.rs`: `ossl_prov_bio_from_dispatch`, whose unit
   `providers/common/bio_prov.c` is not transcribed because this crate builds no provider-side
   `BIO_METHOD`; `ossl_bio_prov_init_bio_method`; and the `corebiometh` setter over it.
8. **Nothing about the three `CT-*` courts' absence from the FRF chain (D413).** They are
   vector-driven and cannot be declared as FRF runtime courts, so **no FRF receipt covers them**.
   Their evidence is `artifacts/phase8/COURTS.json` and
   `forensics/tools/correctness_vectors.py`. §8 states this as a limitation of the instrument rather
   than of the courts.
9. **Nothing about behaviour the divergences of §5 record**, in either direction. Where the authority
   aborts, this crate answers; where the authority is perlasm, this crate is portable; where the
   divergence is the random layer's, this crate refuses.
10. **Every count is `docs/SEAL-CENSUS.md`'s.** This document types none, except §3's per-court table,
    which names the file it was read from.

Fifteen `(id, phase, probe, description)` rows were added to

each naming the `artifacts/phase8/probes/<probe>.{authority,candidate}` pair the court stages and the
`courts/phase8/<probe>.c` it was compiled from. `python3 forensics/tools/gen_frf_courts.py` wrote the
declaration files and `--check` moved from `124 file(s) match the table (62 courts)` to
`154 file(s) match the table (77 courts)`; it is run in CI and in the pipeline, and it passes. The
declarations live at `forensics/frf/courts/openssl-rs-rt-{digest,cipher,cipher-mem,rsa,dh,dsa,ec,ameth,pem-key,pubkey,ecx,keymgmt,signature,asym-cipher,provider-cap}`.
`forensics/frf/run_courts.sh` derives the courts it runs by globbing that directory, so a court added
here is run by construction rather than by a list someone has to remember.

**The three `CT-*` courts are not declared, and the reason is the instrument.** `CT-DIGEST`,
`CT-CIPHER` and `CT-ML-DSA` are vector-driven: `forensics/tools/correctness_vectors.py` compiles each
probe once against the candidate alone and compares its output with committed expected bytes. Such a
court has **no authority transcript to diff, no staged `{authority,candidate}` pair and no fixture a
challenge could locate** (D13, D201). A manifest generated from the table would name execution-context
artifacts that do not exist and would fail at admission, so the table's Phase 8 block declares the
fifteen and records the three-name omission with its reason rather than fabricating rows. **What
follows is a limitation of the FRF instrument, not a weakening of the courts**: the three still run in
the pipeline, and §3's counts for them are their own.

### The chain

The chain ran in the FRF tooling container (`bash docker/openssl-rs-frf-court.sh up`/`exec`), never on
the host, and **without** the `rm -rf` that `forensics/frf/run_courts.sh` begins with: `.frf/` is
committed evidence and this stratum **added** to it, exactly as D200 did for Phase 7. The
invocations are the ones `run_courts.sh` makes — `court run`, `receipt emit`, `court challenge`,
`claim compile --policy sensitivity-backed`.

**Fifteen receipts**, one per declared court, every one emitted with a clean residual count in the
second pass. **Thirty challenge records**, both declared axes on every court, every one adjudicated
with `saw_defect: true` and `specificity_clean: true` and none refused — which is the property (D13)
that makes a court's verdict *sensitivity-backed* rather than merely green.

### The claim, and why there are two

Two `sensitivity-backed` claims exist for this stratum, and the difference between them is the whole
of §4's last finding:

- **`642866a3…`** was compiled in the first pass over the fifteen receipts. It binds authority
  `openssl-rt-3.6.4-r2` to candidate `openssl-rs 0.0.11 (e4f60d8b)` with **zero blockers**, and
  **fourteen** of its premises assert "preserves *<family>* first stdout line **and** *<family>* exit
  class" while `RT-EC`'s cell carries **only its exit class**. That narrowing is not an accident of
  the compiler: `RT-EC`'s real run had raised a residual on its stdout surface, FRF admits a claim over
  each premise's *clean* surface, and so it narrowed the cell rather than the claim.
- **`40c229c5…`** is the corrected claim, and it is **stronger**: all **fifteen** premises — `rt-ec`
  among them — assert both axes, with zero blockers and no excluded evidence. It could only be
  emitted once the residual was dispositioned, and getting there took a measurement of the compiler
  itself.

### The residual, and the disposition that is not a disappearance

`RT-EC`'s first real run diverged on exactly one observation of a 2,420-line transcript:
`short.tail=0,1` against the authority and `short.tail=26,1` against the candidate. The cause is the
**probe**: `courts/phase8/rt_ec_probe.c` printed `one[1]` of an uninitialised array as a sentinel its
comment described and its code never set. The value was environment-determined and never
library-determined — `0` on both sides in the court venue, `0` against `26` in FRF's — and the
candidate library was identical throughout. That is §4's D413.

The probe is fixed. The claim could not simply be recompiled, and the reason is worth recording
because it is counter-intuitive: **a clean receipt makes the claim harder, not easier.** The corrected
receipt's clean surface is the full `{stdout, exit}`, so the claim's `RT-EC` cell *widens* onto the
surface the pre-fix residual occupies, and the pre-fix residual still intersects it. So the receipt
has to be paired with a **disposition**.

The disposition is not a free choice, and it is not chosen for its convenience. FRF's own compiler
names the blocking set in source rather than in prose — `is_scope_blocking` in
`frf/src/commands/claim.rs` matches `open | unknown | nonreproduced | stabilized`, with the comment
that `fixed` is the *only* remediation-evidence state and that `nonreproduced` and `stabilized`
observe disappearance **without a candidate change**, which is "real evidence of nondeterminism, never
a license to claim compatibility". Of the eight settable dispositions:

- `nonreproduced` was applied first and **refused as a resolution**, which is how the rule above was
  measured rather than assumed;
- `fixed` requires a resolution run under a **changed candidate artifact**, and the candidate never
  changed — that is the whole point of the finding;
- `intentional` and `oracle_version` would both be false statements about what happened;
- **`environmental`** is the one that describes the mechanism, because the value came from the
  execution envelope.

The residual therefore moves `open → nonreproduced → environmental`, each transition carrying a
reason, and the `environmental` reason states the probe defect, the venue-dependence and the
candidate's unchanged identity outright. **The pre-fix capture, receipt and residual all remain in the
store**, with their original content addresses, as the record of what the earlier tooling observed.
Nothing was removed and nothing was rewritten.

## 10. Corrections this seal records

Appended rather than folded into the sections above, for the reason the Phase 5 seal's §9 gives: a
correction that has been merged into the prose it corrects cannot be checked against the prose it
replaced.

1. **"`provctx` is NULL" (D411).** The digest half's header stated, from the bootstrap, that the
   `provctx` a provider function is handed was NULL. It was not, and the claim is retracted where it
   stood — `ossl_default_provider_init` builds it with `ossl_prov_ctx_new` and publishes it — and the
   arms that now read it (`deflt_get_params`, the capability walk) would have been reading a context
   the documentation said did not exist.

   they were absent *because no ledger owns them*. That reasoning was right and its conclusion was the
